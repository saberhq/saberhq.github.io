My name is Saber. I work where AI meets biology — for the past year at Genentech (gRED), my day job was co-leading the analysis of two genome-wide, multi-million-cell Perturb-seq screens. The question that organized that work has stuck with me: not *which genes moved*, but whether the relationships we infer can be trusted on biology nobody has measured yet.

Before that, a decade of building tools for reading RNA. My [PhD](https://open.library.ubc.ca/soa/cIRcle/collections/ubctheses/24/items/1.0444844) at UBC and the BC Cancer Genome Sciences Centre produced [ntEmbd](/blog/ntEmbd/), a deep-learning embedding for nucleotide sequences, and [NanoSim](/blog/nanosim/), a long-read simulator the field uses for benchmarking. That work made stops in Nature Methods, Nature Communications, and GigaScience — but mostly, I like building tools that outlive their papers.

### Now

**Sidechain** — my solo entry in the Virtual Cell Challenge 2026, and my bet that the post-transcriptional layer — how RNA-binding proteins and microRNAs govern transcript fate, written in sequence and stable across cell contexts — is a prior most perturbation-response models leave on the table. Writeup coming.

**This site** — my notebook in public. Notes, videos, and the occasional experiment. And yes: `sh` is both my initials and the oldest command in computing. The favicon knows.
